About

Hi, I am Morita.

I am finishing a PhD in Delft on conversational AI and human reflection, and starting a postdoc here soon. Before that I was an NLP researcher and a data scientist. I also make data science tutorials on YouTube, in Persian, because there is not much of this around in Persian.

The short version

I did my bachelor's in Information Technology Engineering and my master's in Computer Engineering, artificial intelligence and robotics, at the University of Tabriz. My master's work was in affective computing, which is where the interest in what machines can and cannot read about a person started. From 2017 I worked as an NLP researcher and a data scientist in industry.

In 2022 I moved to the Netherlands to join the Interactive Intelligence group at TU Delft, supervised by Catharine Oertel, Hayley Hung and Catholijn Jonker, and became part of the Hybrid Intelligence consortium. My doctoral work has been around a single idea. People and machines are good at different halves of this. A system can see the shape of a reflection but cannot recover why the gaps are there, and the person may know the reasons without ever seeing the pattern. Neither half is enough on its own. I research how the two intelligences can be brought together around a person’s own thinking, a “hybrid intelligence” approach.

Why decisions

Moving countries is a decision you make once with incomplete information and then live inside for years. So is leaving a job, or staying in one. What struck me at the time was not that the choice was hard. It was that I kept revisiting the same three considerations and never once looked at the fourth. Nobody could tell me what to choose, and I did not want them to. What would have helped was someone holding up a mirror to the shape of the thinking itself.

That is what four papers have been building toward. The agent does the half a person cannot do mid-conversation, tracking how attention has been distributed across different considerations. It sees, for example, that someone has covered the practical side of a decision at length and the emotional side barely at all. Across several studies I look at whether and how bringing the two intelligences together, human and artificial, shapes the trajectory of a person’s thinking and how they experience it.

Why a YouTube channel

Morita DataLand started because learning data science in Persian meant piecing together fragments, and learning it in English meant assuming a background that a lot of talented students simply do not have access to. So I made the bridge I needed. It is the least academic thing I do and possibly the one with the widest reach.

What is next

I defend in 2027. After that I want to keep doing research on how AI supports human thinking, as a postdoc at TU Delft, and to extend my line of work from one-person decisions to group deliberation.
