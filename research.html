Research

Structure from the machine, meaning from the human.

My doctoral work asks a narrow version of a large question. When an AI system can see the shape of your thinking but not its content, what should it be allowed to do with that? My answer across four papers is: surface it, do not steer with it.

The problem

People making a high-stakes personal decision, a career move, a relocation, a relationship, tend to circle a few considerations and leave others untouched. A conversational agent can measure that. It can count how broadly a person has ranged across analytical, emotional and experiential grounds, and how deeply they have gone into each. What it cannot do is know why a gap is there. Silence about money can mean the money is settled, or that it is unbearable to look at.

That gap between an observable pattern and its meaning is the centre of my thesis. I call it the interpretive gap. The design principle I argue for follows from it directly. An agent should make its observation visible and let the person say what it means, rather than acting on its own reading. The next step is moving this from one person deciding alone to groups of people with differing perspectives, where each person has to scaffold their own reasoning while finding common ground with the others.

  • EMNLP Findings2026

    Why This and Not That? A Collaborative Reflection Approach for Understanding Thought Coverage in Decision Making Support Dialog

    Morita Tarvirdians, Hayley Hung, Catharine Oertel

    Interpret Why is a gap in someone's thinking there?

    The first empirical characterisation of the interpretive gap, the distance between a pattern an agent observes and the reason behind it. It contributes a nine-category taxonomy of the reasons people give for their own coverage patterns. The same observable state produced eight different reasons, so an agent cannot read meaning from behaviour alone. When people steer, they disagree with the agent about direction, not about broadening.

    Paper

  • ACM UMAP2026

    Reflecti-Mate: A Conversational Agent for Adaptive Decision-Making Support Through System 1 and System 2 Thinking

    Morita Tarvirdians, Senthil Chandrasegaran, Hayley Hung, Catholijn M. Jonker, Catharine Oertel

    Adapt Can an agent model a reflection as it unfolds and adapt to it?

    An agent that models how a person spreads attention across internal, external and experiential considerations, then adapts its questions to broaden and deepen reflection. Compared with the same language model without this profile, it supported more holistic, integrative reflection while keeping each person's own way of thinking instead of pulling everyone toward cognition.

    Paper

  • Preprint2025

    Reflection Before Action: Designing a Framework for Quantifying Thought Patterns for Increased Self-awareness in Personal Decision Making

    Morita Tarvirdians, Senthil Chandrasegaran, Hayley Hung, Catholijn M. Jonker, Catharine Oertel

    Measure How do you measure reflection without judging what was said?

    A framework that turns an open reflection into measurable breadth and depth across seven categories, with reliable coding (κ = .69 to .79). It shows that people rate their own reflection higher than it measures, which is the gap the later agents are built to close.

    Paper
